Safer AI use

Before you paste: remove what the AI does not need.

A useful prompt needs context, but it rarely needs real names, contact details, account numbers or confidential records. This guide shows how to keep the useful context and remove unnecessary risk.

The 30-second check

  1. Who could this identify? Remove names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, dates of birth and unusual identifiers.
  2. What could cause harm if exposed? Remove passwords, access keys, payment details, health information and private case notes.
  3. Is the exact detail necessary? Replace it with a role, broad range, category or fictional placeholder.
  4. Is this an approved tool? Follow your organisation's AI, data-handling and confidentiality rules.
Check a draft prompt

Start with the task, not the source material

Tell the AI what outcome you want before pasting any text. Often you can describe the task and provide a short, de-identified example instead of sharing the original document.

Instead of: “Rewrite this email from Jane Smith at 14 Example Street about invoice 48291.”

Try: “Rewrite a polite email from a customer who is querying an overdue invoice. Keep the tone calm and concise.”

Use placeholders consistently

Replace identifying details with clear labels such as [CUSTOMER], [COLLEAGUE], [DATE] or [REFERENCE]. Consistent placeholders preserve the relationships in the text, so the AI can still understand who did what.

Original idea: A named employee missed a meeting with a named client on a specific date.

Safer version: “[EMPLOYEE] missed a scheduled meeting with [CLIENT]. Draft a brief apology and propose two new times.”

Generalise numbers and dates

Exact values can identify a person or transaction. Use an age band instead of a birth date, a rounded amount instead of an account balance, or “last week” instead of an exact appointment date—unless precision is essential to the task.

Keep high-risk material out

Do not paste passwords, one-time codes, API keys, card details, bank details or login credentials into a prompt. Treat health, safeguarding, legal, disciplinary and financial records with particular care. If your work requires sensitive material, use only systems and processes explicitly approved for it.

Summarise confidential documents

If you want help with a private document, write a neutral summary containing only the facts needed for the task. You can also ask for a template first, then fill in private details outside the AI tool.

Template-first prompt: “Create a six-section structure for a factual incident report. Include headings and neutral questions, but no example personal data.”

Review the output too

Removing sensitive input is only half the job. Check the response for invented facts, unintended disclosure, unsuitable advice and details copied from your prompt. A polished answer still needs human review.

A reusable prompt pattern

“Help me [TASK] for [AUDIENCE]. The relevant context is [MINIMUM DE-IDENTIFIED CONTEXT]. Use a [TONE] tone and return [FORMAT]. Do not invent missing facts; mark any assumptions.”
